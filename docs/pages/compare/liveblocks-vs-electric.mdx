Liveblocks is a realtime collaboration platform that allows you to build
multiplayer applications where users and agents can work together. It provides a
sync engine that powers shared documents, text editing, presence, and more.
Electric Sync (ElectricSQL) is a read-path sync engine that streams subsets of
Postgres data, called shapes, to clients over HTTP, while writes go through your
own API.

<Banner title="Electric Cloud is shutting down" type="warning">
  Electric was acquired in 2026 and [its cloud services are winding
  down](https://electric.ax/blog/2026/08/11/electric-joining-databricks#what-this-means-for-you).
</Banner>

## Which should you choose? [#which-should-you-choose]

Electric and Liveblocks solve different problems. Electric is how Postgres rows
reach your clients, whereas Liveblocks is how people edit artifacts together.

Choose Liveblocks when several people work on the same canvas, document, or
workflow at once, and their edits need to merge without overwriting each other.
Choose Electric when Postgres is your source of truth and clients need a live
local copy of selected rows, such as an issue list that updates as assignments
change, while writes keep going through your existing API. Issue trackers often
use both: business records in Postgres delivered by Electric, and the editable
specification in a Liveblocks document.

## Follow a change from the client back to the client [#follow-a-change-from-the-client-back-to-the-client]

Electric only handles the read path, so the write route stays with your
application. Liveblocks applies edits to a shared document and sends them on.
The two paths look like this:

| Stage                 | Electric-based application                                   | Liveblocks document                                          |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| User makes a change   | Your app applies an optimistic update locally.               | Storage or an editor integration applies the edit locally.   |
| Source-of-truth write | Your API validates the write and commits it to Postgres.     | The Liveblocks server applies the operation to the document. |
| Other clients update  | Electric streams the changed rows to every subscribed shape. | Liveblocks sends the change to everyone in the room.         |
| Reporting             | Query Postgres directly.                                     | Read the document, or keep a copy in your own database.      |

Electric's [write guide](https://electric-sql.com/docs/guides/writes) covers
online writes, optimistic updates, and persistent local state. What users see
while a write is pending is up to your implementation. In
[Liveblocks Storage](/docs/products/sync/storage), the local edit shows
immediately and the server confirms it.

## Does Electric handle writes back to Postgres? [#does-electric-handle-writes-back-to-postgres]

No. Electric is a read-path sync engine: it streams data out of Postgres but
does not write into it. Its
[write guide](https://electric-sql.com/docs/guides/writes) documents several
patterns, including online writes and persistent optimistic state, but your
application builds and runs that write path.

For an issue tracker, you might keep accounts, issues, and assignments in
Postgres, use Electric to deliver the relevant rows, and keep your existing API
for updates. Check the full loop from a local edit through validation to the
updated row arriving back at the client.

For a shared canvas, start with the operations on shapes, lists, and text. Check
whether Liveblocks Storage represents those interactions directly, then decide
how that document connects to your business records.

## Can Electric work with Yjs? [#can-electric-work-with-yjs]

Yes, by adding Yjs yourself. Syncing a row that contains a paragraph does not
define how two people's edits to that paragraph merge. Electric publishes
[examples that combine its sync with Yjs](https://electric-sql.com/docs/integrations/yjs):
Yjs merges the text, and Electric carries the Yjs updates through Postgres.
Liveblocks ships
[Yjs-based editor integrations](/docs/products/sync/text-editing) that handle
the same merging without a separate transport.

With either design, decide which representation of the content is the source of
truth. If you also keep a searchable or reportable copy, define how that copy
updates and how much delay your app can tolerate.

## Pricing and deployment [#pricing-and-deployment]

[Electric Cloud](https://electric-sql.com/pricing) charges for writes and
retention; reads, egress, and concurrent users are free. Pay-as-you-go is $1 per
million writes and $0.10 per GB-month of retention, with monthly bills under $5
waived. Live Postgres changes add $2 per million writes on top. Pro is $249 per
month, which acts as prepaid usage credit at a 10% discount. Your Postgres
hosting, your write API, and any editor layer such as Yjs come on top of that.

For Liveblocks, include the collaboration usage plus your application back end.
Your [plan's credits](/docs/pricing/plans) apply once across all Liveblocks
usage.

Electric is open source and can be
[self-hosted](https://electric-sql.com/docs/guides/deployment) alongside your
Postgres. Liveblocks offers
[self-hosting](/docs/platform/data-storage#Self-hosting) as a paid add-on on the
Enterprise plan. Either way, your own servers, database, backups, and operations
are part of the budget.
